Pack mesh

Six aircraft, one operator, and now all six are working. A peer-to-peer mesh lets every drone share what its sensors see and decide, with no leader, which of them should verify a detection.

Baseline versus mesh

The GMCS can route the whole pack but can actively control one drone in mission mode. The other drones carry sensors and a mission computer and use neither: the single-drone GMCS bottleneck. The mesh exploits the drones while they're under pack routing. They scan, share and nominate verifiers on their own. The operator keeps full control of the one drone they fly.

Wide-area scanning

Every drone keeps its direction-finder and imagers running on the route and files each reading with the pack, an imager’s once a second look confirms it.

Cooperative triangulation

Bearings from several drones intersect by weighted least squares. Near-parallel geometry is rejected.

Mission task prioritisation

Every event gets an urgency from class, novelty, confidence and freshness. All drones compute the same ranking.

Common learning of the situation

One shared table of benign signatures. An operator dismissal reaches every drone, and the drone checking it is released.

Verification and alerting

The best-placed drones approach from different angles. Two sensor types must agree before a PRIORITY alert.

What happens, second by second

10 sPre-briefed weather radio detected; urgency 0.08, ignored.
20.0 sBeacon switches on. D1 is 37 km away, out of DF range.
22.0 sThree bearings: localised, CEP 913 m. D2 (EO, from the east) and D4 (IR, from the south-south-east) nominated. D5, second-nearest, is excluded for fuel.
31.7 sFive bearings, held for 10 s: ADVISORY sent by D2 over LOS. CEP 604 m, 1.8 km off: D2’s own bearing was in a reflection.
90 sFault injected: D2 is lost.
95.1 sD4 takes the primary role. With one drone fewer the diversion budget allows one verifier.
100 sNovel ISM emitter appears behind a hill (urgency 0.41). It waits behind the beacon.
151.8 sD4’s IR, confirmed over two looks, and the bearings agree: CEP 69 m, actual error 24 m. PRIORITY sent over LEO SATCOM. D4 moves on to the ISM emitter.
420 sOperator dismisses the ISM emitter through D1. Every drone learns it; D4 is released.

The two minutes between the alerts are flight time: D4 had to close to within its IR’s 10 km range. The mesh does not shorten the transit. It starts the right drones at once, from an estimate with a CEP under a kilometre, and informs the operator meanwhile.

Is the position estimate honest?

A 20,000-trial Monte Carlo at 2.5 degrees of bearing error shows about half of fixes fall inside the reported CEP, as a CEP should (49.9 to 51.5 per cent). Poor geometry has heavy tails: 2 to 6 per cent of fixes are more than three times the CEP off. Multipath makes the tails heavier, which is why a bearing-only warning needs three bearings held for 10 s, and PRIORITY a second sensor type that the bearings confirm on their own.

Over 200 runs

The storyline was repeated 200 times with every random process reseeded, and each source moved by up to 3 km and 10 s. An early warning came in 99.5 % of runs (median 11.5 s) and a PRIORITY in 98 % (median 224 s, position error 50 m). No run raised a false PRIORITY. False early warnings came at 0.09 per drone-hour, every one a real source misplaced by multipath. The 16-drone scenario, with two beacons on one frequency, a fire, clutter, a partition and a lost verifier, also had no false PRIORITY in 200 runs.

How the pack decides

Pack independence: no leader. Every drone folds the same readings into the same table and computes the same plan. Drones with the same table agree without negotiating.

  1. Share. Each drone broadcasts a heartbeat at 1 Hz (position, fuel, active sensor, mode, task) and each sensor reading as it happens; an imager’s once a second look confirms it. Own readings are re-sent every 2 s so a lost packet only delays convergence.
  2. Fuse. Every refresh re-clusters all readings in a canonical order, so the grouping and the event ids depend only on the readings, not on arrival order. Bearings intersect into a position; an event takes the lowest id its readings carry.
  3. Rank. evaluateAndCoordinateAlerts() runs every 0.5 s on every drone and ranks events by urgency.
  4. Nominate. For each event the best-fitting free drones are chosen. A drone that finds itself in the plan sends a request to the FCS and claims its slot on the mesh. Claims are arbitrated by quantised score, ties by lowest id, so the holder never depends on arrival order.
  5. Alert. ADVISORY once an event is localised well enough, PRIORITY once two sensor types agree. One drone is the owner, with ranked backups. At the GMCS an alert consumer folds duplicates by id and by place, tracks acknowledgement and sends dismissals back to the pack.

Hard limits and alert levels

eligible:  PACK_ROUTING only
           (ACTIVE_MISSION, RTB never)
fuel:      endurance - bingo
           - (2 x ETA + 300 s) >= 0
corridor:  standoff inside the approved area
budget:    at most floor(0.4 x free drones),
           minimum 1
verifiers: 2 if urgency >= 0.6, else 1

ADVISORY:  severity >= 0.7, confidence >= 0.6,
           CEP <= 2 km; from bearings alone,
           >= 3 bearings, held for 10 s
PRIORITY:  confidence >= 0.9, CEP <= 250 m,
           >= 2 observers, >= 2 sensor types;
           >= 3 bearings agree on their own

Confidence is a noisy-OR of the readings, capped at 0.85 for one sensor type and 0.99 for two or more. A single sensor type can never confirm an event.

Embedded C++17

Flight-library rules: no exceptions or RTTI, no heap allocation after construction, bounded loops, explicit little-endian wire format. make audit fails the build if the library uses new, throw, std::vector and similar. One PackMeshManager is about 128 KB of fixed storage.

Not done yet

Read these before relying on any of this.

  • The cipher is not a validated module.Frames are sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305, implemented from RFC 8439 and vector-tested. A fielded system needs a validated module behind the same interface.
  • Insiders can forge within an epoch.Keys are symmetric. A captured drone can forge frames in any member's name until the GMCS revokes it and re-keys the pack without it.
  • No flight validation.Every performance figure comes from a simulation. Its radio, terrain, multipath and clutter models are generic, not measured on the real radio, DF or airframe.
  • The mesh does not relay.Every frame is one hop. A pack wider than the radio range splits into groups that each alert on their own.
  • The 8270's real interfaces are assumed.All hardware access goes through one abstraction layer. The buses in the diagram are recommendations.
  • The FCS interface has no reply.A real one needs accept and refuse.
  • Time assumes a shared GNSS-disciplined clock.GNSS-denied operation is not handled.
  • The corridor is a circle.A real approved corridor is a polygon.
  • A pack member can still lie in its own name.Authentication proves who sent a frame, not that it is true. Content checks limit the damage and revocation removes the drone.
  • Persistent clutter is not modelled.One-frame false detections are removed by a second look. A false detection that persists, such as a sun-heated rock, could still raise an ADVISORY, and with bearings that happen to cross near it, a PRIORITY.
  • A partition doubles the alerts.Each side of a split alerts under its own id. After the heal the pack converges on one event and stays quiet; the GMCS alert consumer folds the second alert into the first record.
The system detects, localises, verifies and alerts. It has no weapons or engagement logic and does not command flight controls: it sends requests to the FCS. Repository: drones-mesh/ with docs, include/, src/, tests/ and sim/.
